Healthy bell pepper plants (Capsicum annuum) produce firm, glossy, thick-walled fruit when provided with proper growing conditions. Peppers are warm-season crops that thrive in heat and require consistent moisture.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY PEPPER PLANTS:
Leaves are dark green, smooth, and slightly waxy without spots or curling. Plants are compact and bushy with strong, upright stems. Flowers are white and star-shaped, producing fruit that develops from green to the mature color (red, yellow, orange, or purple depending on variety). Healthy root systems are fibrous and well-developed.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING PEPPER HEALTH:
1. Planting: Transplant after soil temperatures reach 65°F and night temperatures stay above 55°F consistently. Space plants 18-24 inches apart in rows 24-36 inches apart. Peppers do not tolerate cold soil or frost.
2. Soil and Nutrition: Well-drained soil with pH 6.2-7.0. Apply balanced fertilizer at planting and side-dress with nitrogen when first fruits set. Avoid excessive nitrogen, which promotes foliage at the expense of fruit.
3. Watering: Provide 1-2 inches of water per week through drip irrigation. Consistent moisture prevents blossom end rot and promotes uniform fruit development. Mulch to conserve moisture and moderate soil temperature.
4. Pest Prevention: Rotate with non-solanaceous crops on a 3-year cycle. Scout for aphids, pepper maggot, European corn borer, and flea beetles. Monitor for bacterial spot, Phytophthora blight, and viral diseases.
5. Harvest: Harvest at green stage or allow to ripen to full color for sweeter flavor and higher vitamin content. Cut rather than pull fruit to avoid damaging plants.
