Healthy soybean plants (Glycine max) are characterized by vigorous vegetative growth, abundant nodulation, and disease-free foliage. Soybeans are a critical crop in the U.S., providing both protein and oil.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY SOYBEAN PLANTS:
Leaves are trifoliate (three leaflets), dark green, and free of spots, discoloration, or feeding damage. Stems are erect and sturdy. Root systems show pink to red nodules indicating active nitrogen fixation by Bradyrhizobium bacteria. Flowers are small, white or purple. Pods develop uniformly and fill completely at maturity.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING SOYBEAN HEALTH:
1. Variety Selection: Choose varieties adapted to your maturity group zone with good disease resistance packages. Consider resistance to soybean cyst nematode (SCN), sudden death syndrome, and Phytophthora root rot.
2. Planting: Plant at optimal dates for your region (late April through May in most of the Corn Belt). Seeding rate of 120,000-140,000 seeds per acre. Plant 1.0-1.5 inches deep in moist soil.
3. Seed Treatment: Use fungicide and insecticide seed treatments to protect early-season stand establishment. Include inoculant if planting in fields without recent soybean history.
4. Weed Management: Implement pre- and post-emergence herbicide programs. Soybeans are poor competitors with weeds, especially during early growth stages.
5. Nutrient Management: Soybeans fix their own nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria. Apply phosphorus and potassium based on soil tests. Monitor for iron deficiency chlorosis (IDC) in high-pH soils.
6. Scouting: Regular field scouting throughout the season for diseases, insects, and nutrient deficiencies is essential for timely management decisions.
