Healthy blueberry plants (Vaccinium corymbosum) are characterized by vigorous growth, abundant fruit production, and disease-free foliage. Blueberries require specific soil conditions and cultural practices to thrive.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY BLUEBERRY PLANTS:
Leaves are medium to dark green, smooth, and oval-shaped without spots or discoloration. Healthy plants produce new canes from the crown annually. Flowers are white to pink bell-shaped clusters. Fruit ripens evenly from green to deep blue with a waxy bloom coating. Root systems are shallow and fibrous.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING BLUEBERRY HEALTH:
1. Soil Requirements: Blueberries require acidic soil with pH 4.5-5.5. Amend soil with sulfur or acidic peat moss before planting. Test soil pH annually and adjust as needed with elemental sulfur.
2. Watering: Provide consistent moisture—1-2 inches per week. Blueberries are sensitive to both drought and waterlogging. Drip irrigation is ideal. Mulch heavily (4-6 inches) with pine bark, sawdust, or pine needles to conserve moisture and maintain acidity.
3. Fertilization: Use ammonium sulfate or fertilizers formulated for acid-loving plants. Apply in early spring and again after bloom. Avoid chloride-containing fertilizers and lime.
4. Pruning: Remove oldest canes (6+ years) at ground level annually. Thin remaining canes to 6-8 per bush. Remove low-growing branches and dead wood. Proper pruning promotes larger fruit and better air circulation.
5. Pest Prevention: Net plants to protect from birds. Monitor for spotted wing drosophila, Japanese beetles, and cranberry fruitworm. Scout for mummy berry, botrytis blight, and Phytophthora root rot.
6. Winter Care: Most highbush varieties are hardy to -20°F. In extreme cold areas, protect with burlap screens or additional mulch around crowns.

Michigan is the leading producer of cultivated blueberries in the United States. Healthy blueberry plantings can remain productive for 40-50 years with proper management. Key factors include maintaining soil acidity, adequate moisture, and controlling major diseases like mummy berry and Phomopsis canker through integrated approaches.
