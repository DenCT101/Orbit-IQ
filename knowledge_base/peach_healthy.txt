Healthy peach trees (Prunus persica) produce abundant, high-quality fruit when properly managed. Peaches require specific cultural practices including proper pruning, pest management, and frost protection.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY PEACH TREES:
Leaves are long, narrow, dark green, and slightly curved without spots, holes, or discoloration. New growth is vigorous with reddish-colored terminals. Bark is smooth and reddish-brown on young wood, becoming rough and dark with age. Fruit develops uniform size and color with firm, juicy flesh. Healthy trees produce 3-6 bushels of fruit annually at maturity.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING PEACH HEALTH:
1. Variety Selection: Choose varieties with adequate chilling hours for your region. Low-chill varieties for the South; high-chill for the North. Disease resistance is an important selection criterion.
2. Pruning: Prune annually to an open-center (vase) shape. This is the most important cultural practice for peaches. Remove 40-50% of previous year's growth to maintain tree size and promote new fruiting wood. Pruning improves air circulation, light penetration, and spray coverage.
3. Thinning: Thin fruit to 6-8 inches apart when marble-sized (4-6 weeks after bloom). Thinning improves fruit size, quality, and prevents branch breakage. Over-cropping weakens trees and promotes biennial bearing.
4. Pest Management: Apply dormant oil and copper spray before budbreak. Monitor for brown rot, bacterial spot, and peach leaf curl. Manage oriental fruit moth, plum curculio, and peachtree borer with appropriate insecticides.
5. Fertilization: Apply 1-2 lbs of 10-10-10 fertilizer per tree age (up to 10 lbs for mature trees) in early spring. Avoid late-season nitrogen applications.
6. Irrigation: Provide 1-2 inches of water per week during fruit development. Reduce watering 2 weeks before harvest to improve fruit quality.
