Healthy cherry trees display vigorous growth with glossy, dark green leaves and abundant fruit production. Both sweet cherries (Prunus avium) and sour cherries (Prunus cerasus) benefit from similar cultural practices.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY CHERRY TREES:
Leaves are oval, serrated, dark green, and glossy without spots or powdery coating. Flowers appear in spring as white to pink clusters. Healthy fruit is firm, uniformly colored, and free of cracks, spots, or mold. Bark is smooth and reddish-brown on young wood, becoming darker and furrowed with age.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING CHERRY HEALTH:
1. Site Selection: Plant in well-drained soil with full sun. Cherries are sensitive to wet feet—avoid low areas or heavy clay soils. Protect from strong winds and late spring frosts.
2. Pollination: Most sweet cherry varieties require a compatible pollinator variety. Sour cherries are generally self-fertile. Ensure pollinators are within 100 feet and bloom simultaneously.
3. Watering: Provide deep, infrequent irrigation during the growing season. Avoid overhead watering to reduce disease pressure. Stop irrigation 2-3 weeks before harvest to prevent fruit cracking.
4. Pruning: Train young trees to an open-center or modified central leader form. Prune in late winter to remove dead, diseased, and crossing branches. Avoid heavy pruning, which can stimulate excessive vegetative growth.
5. Disease Prevention: Apply dormant sprays of copper or lime-sulfur to reduce overwintering pathogens. Monitor for bacterial canker, brown rot, and powdery mildew throughout the growing season.
6. Bird Protection: Net trees when fruit begins to color. Birds can devastate cherry crops in a single day.

Maintaining cherry tree health depends on proper rootstock selection, orchard floor management, and timely pest management. Modern cherry orchards increasingly use dwarfing rootstocks that allow for more intensive management and earlier production.
