Healthy tomato plants (Solanum lycopersicum) are the most widely grown home garden vegetable. Maintaining plant health requires attention to variety selection, cultural practices, and pest management.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY TOMATO PLANTS:
Leaves are compound, dark green, and slightly aromatic when brushed. Stems are sturdy and hairy (pubescent). Plants grow vigorously with balanced vegetative and reproductive growth. Flowers are yellow and develop into fruit that ripens uniformly. Root systems are deep and extensive, anchoring plants firmly.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING TOMATO HEALTH:
1. Variety Selection: Choose varieties adapted to your region with disease resistance packages (VFN, TSWV, etc.). Determine whether determinate or indeterminate types suit your needs.
2. Transplanting: Set transplants deep — bury stem up to the first true leaves to promote adventitious root formation. Space plants 24-36 inches apart with 36-48 inches between rows.
3. Support: Stake, cage, or trellis plants to keep foliage off the ground. This reduces disease spread and improves air circulation. Prune suckers on staked plants for better air flow.
4. Watering: Provide 1-2 inches of water per week through consistent, deep irrigation. Avoid wetting foliage. Irregular watering causes blossom end rot, cracking, and catfacing.
5. Mulching: Apply 2-3 inches of organic mulch after soil warms to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and prevent soil splash that spreads disease.
6. Fertilization: Side-dress with nitrogen after first fruit set. Avoid excessive nitrogen early, which promotes vegetative growth at the expense of fruit. Monitor for calcium, magnesium, and potassium deficiencies.
7. Pest and Disease Monitoring: Scout regularly for hornworms, aphids, whiteflies, and early blight, late blight, septoria leaf spot, and bacterial diseases. Remove affected tissue promptly.

Healthy tomato production integrates genetic resistance, cultural practices, biological control, and targeted chemical applications. Consistent monitoring and early intervention are keys to maintaining plant health throughout the growing season.
