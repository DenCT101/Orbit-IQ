Healthy grapevines exhibit vigorous, balanced growth with clean foliage and high-quality fruit production. Maintaining vine health requires attention to vineyard site selection, variety choice, training systems, and ongoing cultural practices.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY GRAPEVINES:
Leaves are large, lobed, medium to dark green, and free of spots, discoloration, or deformity. Shoot growth is vigorous and balanced — not excessively vegetative or stunted. Tendrils are firm and actively grasping. Fruit clusters develop evenly with uniform berry size and color at maturity. Trunk and cordons are smooth without cankers, gumming, or discolored bark.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING GRAPEVINE HEALTH:
1. Site and Variety Selection: Choose varieties suited to your climate and soil conditions. Good air drainage minimizes frost risk and reduces disease pressure. Well-drained soils prevent root rot.
2. Training and Pruning: Use appropriate training systems (Geneva Double Curtain, VSP, or bilateral cordon) for your variety and climate. Annual dormant pruning maintains vine balance and ensures adequate sunlight and air circulation through the canopy.
3. Canopy Management: Shoot position, thin, and hedging during the growing season maintain an open canopy. Leaf removal in the fruit zone improves spray penetration and reduces disease risk.
4. Irrigation: Manage water carefully — vines need adequate moisture for growth but excessive irrigation promotes disease and reduces fruit quality. Deficit irrigation strategies can improve wine grape quality.
5. Nutrient Management: Base fertilization on soil and tissue analysis. Potassium is particularly important for grape quality. Avoid excessive nitrogen, which promotes excessive vegetative growth.
6. Integrated Pest Management: Monitor regularly for insects, diseases, and nutritional disorders. Use economic thresholds and combine cultural, biological, and chemical controls as needed.
