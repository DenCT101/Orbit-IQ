SOURCE: University of California — Strawberry Production Guidelines
URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/strawberry/

Healthy strawberry plants (Fragaria × ananassa) produce abundant, high-quality fruit when properly managed. Strawberries require specific cultural practices including proper planting density, irrigation management, and disease prevention.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY STRAWBERRY PLANTS:
Leaves are compound with three leaflets, dark green, glossy, and free of spots or discoloration. Plants produce multiple crowns with vigorous runner production (in June-bearing types). Flowers are white with yellow centers, developing into firm, uniformly red fruit with excellent flavor and aroma. Root systems are fibrous and well-developed.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING STRAWBERRY HEALTH:
1. Planting Systems: Choose between matted-row (June-bearing), hill system (day-neutral), or plasticulture based on your climate and goals. Plant in early spring in northern regions or fall in southern regions.
2. Site Preparation: Well-drained soil with pH 6.0-6.5. Amend with organic matter before planting. Avoid sites where tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, or strawberries grew within the past 3-5 years to prevent Verticillium wilt.
3. Watering: Provide 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Use drip irrigation to keep foliage dry and reduce disease pressure. Consistent moisture during fruit development prevents misshapen fruit.
4. Fertilization: Apply balanced fertilizer at planting and side-dress with nitrogen during the growing season. Avoid excessive nitrogen, which promotes soft fruit susceptible to rot.
5. Mulching: Apply 3-4 inches of straw mulch after the ground freezes in fall for winter protection. In spring, pull mulch back from plants to allow growth, keeping it between rows for weed suppression and fruit protection.
6. Renovation: For matted-row June-bearers, renovate immediately after harvest — mow tops, narrow rows, thin plants, fertilize, and irrigate to promote healthy new growth for the next season.
