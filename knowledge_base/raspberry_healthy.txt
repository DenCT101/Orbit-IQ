Healthy raspberry plants (Rubus idaeus) produce abundant crops of high-quality fruit when properly managed. Raspberries require specific cultural practices to maintain vigor and prevent disease.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY RASPBERRY PLANTS:
Canes are vigorous, upright, and evenly spaced. First-year canes (primocanes) are green and produce leaves. Second-year canes (floricanes) produce fruit and then die. Leaves are compound with 3-5 leaflets, dark green on top and silvery-white beneath. Fruit is firm, uniformly colored, and separates easily from the receptacle at harvest.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING RASPBERRY HEALTH:
1. Site Preparation: Plant in well-drained, fertile soil with full sun. Good air circulation is essential. Avoid sites where tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, or other raspberries grew in the past 3-5 years to prevent Verticillium wilt and Phytophthora root rot.
2. Pruning: For summer-bearing types, remove spent floricanes immediately after harvest. Thin primocanes to 4-6 per foot of row. For fall-bearing types, mow all canes to ground level in late winter for a single fall crop, or selectively remove spent floricanes for both summer and fall crops.
3. Trellising: Support canes with a T-trellis or V-trellis system. This keeps canes upright, improves air circulation, and facilitates harvest.
4. Irrigation: Provide 1-2 inches of water per week during fruiting season. Use drip irrigation to keep foliage dry. Mulch with straw or wood chips to conserve moisture.
5. Disease Prevention: Remove and destroy diseased canes promptly. Apply dormant lime-sulfur to reduce overwintering pathogens. Monitor for gray mold (Botrytis), cane blight, anthracnose, and root rot.
