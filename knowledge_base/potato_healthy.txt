Healthy potato plants (Solanum tuberosum) are the foundation of productive potato production. Proper cultural practices from planting through harvest ensure plant health and maximize tuber yield and quality.

CHARACTERISTICS OF HEALTHY POTATO PLANTS:
Foliage is uniformly dark green and upright. Leaves are compound with multiple leaflets, free of spots, lesions, or discoloration. Stems are sturdy and self-supporting. Flower clusters appear at the top of plants, typically white or purple depending on variety. Below ground, tubers develop uniformly with smooth skin, shallow eyes, and firm, white flesh. Healthy root systems support vigorous above-ground growth.

BEST PRACTICES FOR MAINTAINING POTATO HEALTH:
1. Seed Selection: Use certified, disease-free seed potatoes. Cut seed pieces to 1.5-2 ounce size with at least one eye each. Allow cut surfaces to heal (suberize) for 1-2 days before planting.
2. Planting: Plant when soil temperatures reach 45-50°F at 4-inch depth. Space seed pieces 10-12 inches apart in rows 30-36 inches apart. Plant 3-4 inches deep and hill progressively as plants grow.
3. Fertility: Apply nitrogen (150-200 lbs N/acre for commercial production) in split applications. Phosphorus and potassium based on soil tests. Adequate potassium improves tuber quality and storage life.
4. Water Management: Maintain consistent soil moisture, especially during tuber initiation and bulking. Avoid water stress and over-watering. Irregular moisture causes growth cracks, hollow heart, and knobby tubers.
5. Pest Monitoring: Scout regularly for Colorado potato beetle, aphids (virus vectors), and flea beetles. Monitor for early blight, late blight, and Verticillium wilt. Use IPM principles for sustainable management.
6. Harvest and Storage: Harvest after vine kill when skin is set and tubers are mature. Cure tubers at 55-60°F with high humidity for 2 weeks to heal wounds. Store at 38-45°F depending on end use.
